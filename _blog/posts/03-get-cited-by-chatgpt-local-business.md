# Your Next Customer Is Asking ChatGPT, Not Google. Here's How to Get Named.

**Short answer:** to get named by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Overviews, you need four things — rank in classic search first, answer real customer questions in the first paragraph of your pages, ship clean structured data and a complete Google Business Profile, and earn corroborating mentions elsewhere on the web. Everything else is decoration. If your business is invisible in AI answers today, one of those four layers is missing, and it's usually the first or the last.

Here's what's changed, what to do about it, and what to ignore.

## What actually changed

People still search. They just don't always click. Questions have turned into conversations, conversations produce answers, and those answers get stitched together across AI systems, search engines, reviews, and brand mentions — often before anyone reaches a website at all.

That flips the goal. Ranking a page still matters. **Being included in the answer matters more.** And the answer usually names two or three businesses, not ten.

For a service business in South Florida, the query that decides your quarter isn't "web design" anymore. It's someone typing *"who should I hire to redesign my restaurant's website in Fort Lauderdale, budget around eight thousand, needs online reservations"* into an assistant and reading whatever comes back.

## The four layers, in priority order

### 1. Rank in classic search first

This is the least exciting and most important item. The overwhelming majority of ChatGPT citations overlap with what already ranks in Bing's top ten, and Google's own guidance is that standard SEO best practice still applies to AI Overviews and AI Mode. Crawlable, indexed, fast, technically sound, genuinely useful.

Two concrete moves most local businesses skip: **submit your site to Bing Webmaster Tools** (ChatGPT Search reads Bing's index — without a presence there, you can't be cited), and confirm you aren't blocking the AI crawlers you actually want citations from.

### 2. Write answers, not keyword pages

AI systems lift chunks. Your job is to make a liftable one.

- Put a **direct, self-contained answer in the first 60–80 words** of every important page. If it can't stand alone when extracted, it won't be extracted.
- Use the customer's actual phrasing as headings. Not "Our Process" — "How long does a website take to build?"
- Include your **business name, city, and service inside the answer block**. When the model lifts the chunk, it lifts your name with it, and the reader gets a business to contact instead of an anonymous fact.
- Write in specifics: numbers, dates, real ranges, named examples. Vague content doesn't get quoted.

### 3. Be machine-legible and consistent

Add `LocalBusiness`, `Organization`, and `FAQPage` schema to your core pages, with `sameAs` linking every profile you own. Keep name, address, and phone identical across every listing — a stale address on a directory you forgot about is an active vote against you.

Then the single highest-leverage local asset, which isn't on your website at all: **your Google Business Profile.** AI Overviews pull categories, service area, hours, and review content straight from it. Complete it, keep it active, and ask happy clients to name the specific service in their review. A dozen detailed reviews outperform fifty generic five-stars, because the systems parse review *content*, not just star counts.

### 4. Get corroborated elsewhere

The model's last question is whether the wider web agrees with you. Community platforms, local roundups, industry directories, podcasts, genuine "best X in [city]" lists, and mentions from partners all build the association between your brand and your category. This is slow and it is the part that can't be faked.

## What to skip

- **llms.txt.** Heavily promoted, weakly evidenced. Google's 2026 guidance says it isn't used for AI Overviews, and there's no solid signal it drives ChatGPT citations either. Ship it if it's free; don't prioritize it.
- **Schema as a rescue.** Structured data helps machines parse good content. It does not make weak content citable — studies looking at commercial queries keep landing on organic rank as the strongest predictor.
- **Checking your "AI rank" daily.** Answers vary run to run. Measure weekly, and measure the thing that pays: calls, quotes, booked work.
- **Mass-produced thin pages.** A page per neighborhood is legitimate if each one says something true and specific. Fifty spun variants read as spam and can actively hurt.
- **"Authoritative tone."** Writing like a white paper showed no measurable lift. Being specific does.

## Why this changes how sites get built

If content has to be liftable, structure stops being a styling concern and becomes an architecture concern. Real semantic HTML. Real headings in question form. FAQ blocks that exist to be parsed. Fast pages so crawlers get through them. Copy written to answer rather than to fill.

## Your 20-minute audit

1. Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Mode who they'd recommend for your service in your city. Write down who gets named.
2. Search your business name. Does the answer describe you accurately?
3. Check Bing Webmaster Tools. Are you indexed at all?
4. Open your Google Business Profile. Complete? Recent reviews? Do they mention specific services?
5. Open your three most important pages. Does each answer a real question in its first paragraph?

Whatever fails there is your actual roadmap.

## FAQ

**How long does it take to start showing up in AI answers?**
For branded queries — "who is [your business]" — typically four to eight weeks once the basics are in place. For competitive category queries like "best web designer in Miami," expect two to five months depending on how entrenched the competition is.

**Is AEO different from SEO?**
It's a layer on top, not a replacement. The foundations overlap almost entirely: crawlability, clear structure, real expertise, credible mentions. What's new is formatting content so it can be extracted and attributed cleanly.

**Do I still need a blog?**
You need pages that answer the questions your customers actually ask. If that takes the shape of a blog, fine. Publishing three unfocused posts a week does nothing — a connected cluster of genuinely useful pages does.

**Does any of this matter for a local business in South Florida specifically?**
More than for national brands, arguably. Local queries lean heavily on Google Business Profile data and review substance, which are entirely within your control and which most of your competitors are neglecting.
