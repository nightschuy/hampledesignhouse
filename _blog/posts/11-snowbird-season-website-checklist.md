# Snowbird Season Website Checklist for South Florida Businesses

**Short answer:** before high season starts in November, make sure your website answers a stranger's first questions in seconds: are you open, where are you, what do you offer, and how do I book or call. Match your hours everywhere, put menus and prices in real text, make booking and calling one tap on a phone, and refresh your reviews. At Hample Design House in Fort Lauderdale, this is the checklist I run on every restaurant and service site I build.

Every fall the same thing happens. The roads get busier, the reservation books fill, and a business that was coasting on regulars suddenly depends on people who have never heard of it. Those people find you on a phone, in a hotel lobby or a rental car, and they decide fast. Your website is the interview.

## Why does high season matter more for your website this year?

More people are landing, and they're arriving from more places.

JetBlue said in July that it expects about **150 daily flights** out of Fort Lauderdale this winter, its largest schedule there, with departures up more than 75% year over year ([JetBlue press release, July 9, 2026](https://news.jetblue.com/latest-news/press-release-details/2026/JetBlue-Launches-Largest-Ever-Fort-Lauderdale-Schedule-Reinforcing-Long-Term-Commitment-to-South-Florida/default.aspx)). New nonstops to Columbus and Indianapolis start November 2, and San Diego follows November 19.

The money is moving too. FIU hospitality dean Michael Cheng [reported](https://news.fiu.edu/2026/fewer-cheap-flights-more-international-suitcases-south-floridas-tourism-outlook-for-2027.html) that Miami-Dade hotels ran nearly **80% occupancy** through May 2026, with average daily rates up **11.2%** to $293.67. His outlook for 2027: visitor counts roughly flat, but spending holding up better. In plain terms, fewer bargain travelers and more people with money who plan ahead.

People who plan ahead research. That's good news if your site does the job, and bad news if it's a logo, a PDF menu, and a phone number that rings out.

## How do visitors choose a local business they've never heard of?

They read reviews, then they check your website. Increasingly they ask an AI first.

[BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/learn/local-consumer-review-survey/) of 1,002 U.S. consumers found:

- **74%** look for reviews written in the last three months, and **32%** want reviews from the last two weeks.
- After reading positive reviews, the most common next step (**54%**) is visiting the business's website.
- Use of ChatGPT and other AI tools for local recommendations jumped from **6% to 45%** in a year.

So the path is: an AI answer or a map listing, a skim of recent reviews, then your website to confirm. If any step contradicts another (different hours, an old menu, a dead booking link), the visitor picks the next option. They have no loyalty to lose.

## What should be on my website before season starts?

Here's the checklist. Most of it is a few hours of work. None of it requires a redesign.

1. **Hours that match everywhere.** Your website, Google Business Profile, Yelp, Apple Maps, and Instagram bio should say the same thing. Add seasonal hours now if they change in November.
2. **Holiday hours set in advance.** Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, Christmas, and New Year's. [Google says](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6333474?hl=en) it may warn customers that your hours "might differ" on holidays unless you set special hours ahead of time.
3. **Menu, services, and prices in real text.** Not a PDF, not a photo of a chalkboard. Text loads fast on hotel Wi-Fi, translates in the browser, and can be read by Google and AI assistants.
4. **One-tap booking or calling.** The reservation, appointment, or quote button sits at the top of the phone screen, and the phone number is a tap-to-call link. Test it on your own phone, on cellular.
5. **Directions and parking.** A visitor doesn't know that the lot is behind the building or that the garage on the corner validates. Say it, with a map link.
6. **A "first time here?" section.** Dress code, walk-ins vs. reservations, kid-friendly or not, how far from the beach. The questions your staff answer on the phone ten times a day.
7. **A season page or banner.** Season menu, snowbird specials, events, holiday parties, gift cards. One page you can update in five minutes, linked from the homepage.
8. **Fresh reviews.** Ask your regulars now, in October and November, so your newest reviews aren't from last spring. Reply to the ones you have.
9. **Spanish, at least for the essentials.** In Miami-Dade and Broward, a Spanish version of your hours, menu, and booking page is not a nice-to-have.
10. **Tracking on the actions that matter.** Calls, bookings, form submissions, and direction clicks should be counted, so in January you know what worked instead of guessing.

## What if my current website can't do this in time?

Then don't try to rebuild everything before Thanksgiving. Triage:

- **Fix the listings first.** Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, and Yelp are where a lot of first contact happens, and you can edit them today.
- **Patch the top of the homepage.** Hours, booking button, phone, address. That's the part a visitor actually sees.
- **Plan the real fix for the shoulder.** May and June are quieter, and a new site that launches before next season has a full year to earn rankings and reviews.

## What happens after a visitor finds me?

The website gets them interested. Speed closes them.

Season is when a missed call or a contact form that sits for six hours costs the most, because the visitor is only in town for a week and has three other tabs open. If you get inquiries after hours, set up an instant text-back or an auto-reply that confirms the request and says when a human will follow up. I covered the setup and costs in [where AI actually pays first for Florida small businesses](/blog/ai-lead-response-small-business-florida/).

A simple test for this week: call your own business at 8 p.m. from a number you don't use, and submit your own contact form. Time how long it takes to hear back. That number is what a visitor experiences.

## The short version

High season is the one stretch of the year when your website is talking mostly to strangers. They don't know your story, they won't dig, and they're comparing you with whoever shows up next in the map pack. Answer their first four questions in the first screen, keep everything consistent, and make the next step one tap. That's the whole game.

## FAQ

**When does snowbird season start in South Florida?**
High season generally runs from November through April, with the biggest crowds from late December through March. Getting your website and listings ready in October gives Google time to pick up the changes before the rush.

**Should my restaurant menu be a PDF or a web page?**
A web page. Text menus load faster on phones, can be translated in the browser, and can be read by Google and AI assistants, while a PDF is often skipped or slow to open on cellular.

**Do I need to set holiday hours on my Google Business Profile?**
Yes, if your hours change. Google says it may show customers a warning that your hours might differ on holidays unless you set special hours in advance, which can cost you visits from people who assume you're closed.

**How recent do my reviews need to be?**
Recent enough to look active. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 74% of consumers look for reviews from the past three months, so a push for new reviews in October and November pays off during season.

**Is it too late to get a new website before season?**
Not for a focused site. A small, well-scoped build can launch in a week or two, but if time is tight, fix your listings and the top of your homepage first and plan the larger rebuild for the quieter summer months.
