# Do I Need a Website if I Have Instagram? The Honest 2026 Answer

**Short answer:** yes, if Instagram is your only home online, you need a website. Instagram is great for being discovered, but you don't own the account, most buyers still verify a business on Google and its own site before paying, and AI search tools mostly read websites, not posts. At Hample Design House in Fort Lauderdale, I build small, fast websites that work alongside Instagram, so the followers you earn have somewhere permanent to land.

That's the short answer. Here's the evidence, and the honest case for when Instagram alone is fine for now.

## What happens to your business if Instagram disables your account?

This isn't hypothetical this week. On October 29, [WKMG News 6 in Orlando reported](https://clickorlando.com/news/local/2026/10/29/orlando-lgbtqia-nightclubs-say-instagram-disabled-accounts-over-human-exploitation-claims) that two Orlando nightclubs had their Instagram accounts disabled, flagged for "human exploitation." One club appealed and, three days later, got a permanent ban. The owners said they couldn't reach a person at Meta. One club's audience fell from about 12,500 followers to about 1,000 on a backup account. Meta later told the station the accounts "were removed in error and have been reinstated."

Reinstated is the good ending. But look at what happened in between: no way to promote that weekend's events, no customer list to fall back on, and a backup account starting from almost nothing.

That's the core problem with running a business only on Instagram. You're building on land you rent, under rules you can't negotiate, enforced by automated systems with slow appeals. A website on your own domain, plus an email list, is the part nobody can switch off for you.

## Do customers trust a business that only has Instagram?

Less than you'd think. Two recent U.S. surveys point the same direction.

In [TrustedFirms' Online Trust Report](https://trustedfirms.io/online-trust-report/), a September 2026 survey of 1,146 U.S. adults:

- **97%** have worried that a business they found online might not be legitimate.
- **87.7%** have decided not to hire or buy because they couldn't find enough information online.
- About **two-thirds** check a business's website when vetting it.

Asked for the single signal they trust most, people picked:

| Trust signal | Share who trust it most |
|---|---|
| Positive online reviews | 55.6% |
| Featured in news or industry media | 20.8% |
| A professional website | 13.0% |
| A strong social media presence | 6.0% |
| Showing up in AI assistant answers | 4.6% |

A website alone won't win the sale. Reviews do the heavy lifting. But a website ranks about twice as high as social media as a trust signal, and the missing website is often why people walk away.

[DreamHost's 2026 Local Business Trust Index](https://www.dreamhost.com/blog/dreamhost-2026-local-business-trust-index/) (a survey of 1,201 Americans in November 2025; note that DreamHost sells websites, so weigh it accordingly) found:

- **58%** often or always check a business's website to confirm something they saw on social media, Google, or an AI tool.
- **39%** have decided not to visit or buy from a business because it had no website.
- People said they were comfortable spending an average of **$177** on a business website, versus **$36** through an Instagram or TikTok shop.

That last number is the one I'd tape to the wall. Social is where people find you. The website is where they feel safe spending real money.

## Can people find you on Google and AI search with only Instagram?

Not reliably. In the same DreamHost survey, **67%** of people start a local business search on Google, and only **19%** of 18 to 24 year olds start on social media. Google can index some Instagram profiles, but a profile with a bio and a grid of photos gives it very little to rank for "[your service] in [your city]."

AI search makes this sharper. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI Mode build answers from pages they can read: services, prices, hours, service areas, written in plain text. A caption buried in a carousel is hard to cite. I wrote about this in detail in [how local businesses get named by ChatGPT](/blog/get-cited-by-chatgpt-local-business/) and [how to get linked in Google AI Mode](/blog/google-ai-mode-website-links/). The short version: AI answers point to the clearest source, and that's almost always a website.

## When don't you need a website if you have Instagram?

I'd rather be fair than sell you something you don't need yet. Instagram alone can be fine if:

- You're testing an idea and don't know if it's a business yet.
- Every customer comes through referrals or DMs and you're already at capacity.
- You sell low-cost, impulse items where nobody checks a website first.
- You'd rather put your first dollars into product or inventory, and you'll revisit in a few months.

Even then, I'd spend 20 minutes today on two free things: claim your Google Business Profile, and start collecting customer emails or phone numbers somewhere you control. If your account disappears tomorrow, those are what's left.

## Website vs. Instagram: what does each one actually do?

| Job | Instagram | Your own website |
|---|---|---|
| Getting discovered by new people | Strong | Moderate, grows with SEO |
| Showing up for "near me" Google searches | Weak | Strong, alongside your Business Profile |
| Getting cited by AI assistants | Weak | Strong when written clearly |
| Proving you're a real, established business | Moderate | Strong |
| Taking bookings, deposits, or orders over $100 | Weak | Strong |
| Owning the audience if the platform changes | No | Yes |
| Cost to start | Free | About $20 to $60 a month DIY, or a one-time professional build |

They're not competitors. Instagram is the shop window on a busy street. The website is the shop.

## What should a small business website have if you already use Instagram?

You don't need 20 pages. For most South Florida small businesses coming from Instagram, this is the whole list:

1. **A homepage that says what you do and where** in the first sentence. "Custom cakes in Fort Lauderdale," not "Welcome to our world."
2. **One page per core service or product line**, with prices or price ranges in text.
3. **Real photos.** Reuse your best Instagram shots. They're already proof of your work.
4. **Reviews**, pulled from Google or quoted with permission.
5. **A booking, order, or contact form** that works in one tap on a phone.
6. **Hours, address or service area, and phone** that match your Google Business Profile exactly.
7. **Your Instagram feed linked or embedded**, so the two work together.
8. **An email or text signup**, so you own a list no platform can take away.
9. **Analytics and conversion tracking**, so you can see what Instagram traffic actually turns into.

## FAQ

**Is Instagram enough for a small business in 2026?**
For testing an idea or a referral-only business, it can be enough for a while. Once you want strangers to find you on Google or AI search, or to pay more than impulse prices, you need a website you own.

**What happens to my customers if my Instagram account gets disabled?**
You lose access to your followers, messages, and posts until an appeal succeeds, which can take days or longer. A website and an email list keep you reachable while you sort it out.

**Can Google show my Instagram in search results?**
Google can index some public Instagram profiles, but a profile gives it little to rank for service and location searches. A website with clear service pages and a Google Business Profile does much better for local searches.

**Do AI assistants like ChatGPT recommend businesses that only have Instagram?**
They can mention them, usually based on reviews and directories. Without a website, the AI has little official information to cite about your services, prices, or hours, so a competitor with a clear site has the advantage.

**How much does a basic small business website cost?**
A DIY or AI builder runs about $20 to $60 a month plus your time. In South Florida, a freelancer build typically lands between $1,500 and $6,000, and a small studio build runs roughly $5,000 to $20,000, depending on pages and features.
