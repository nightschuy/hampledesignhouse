# How to Claim Your Business on ChatGPT (Business Hub, Nov 2026)

**Short answer:** OpenAI's Business Hub, launched in beta on November 10, 2026, lets you claim or create your business profile in ChatGPT, verify your domain and identity, edit your details, and see how often ChatGPT mentions you. Claiming is free and worth doing, but OpenAI says it doesn't guarantee better placement. At Hample Design House in Fort Lauderdale, I treat it as one more listing to match against a website that does the real persuading.

Here's what launched, who can use it today, the setup steps, and where it fits next to your Google Business Profile and your site.

## What is ChatGPT Business Hub?

On November 10, OpenAI [announced Businesses in ChatGPT in beta](https://community.openai.com/t/introducing-businesses-in-chatgpt-in-beta/1414865). The pitch, in OpenAI's words: "Claim your business, manage your information, and get insights on how people engage with you." Business Hub is the dashboard where that happens.

It does four things:

1. **Claim or create a profile.** Search for your business by name. If ChatGPT already has a listing for you, claim it. If not, create one.
2. **Manage your details.** Description, category, contact info, and (for online sellers) policy answers about delivery and returns.
3. **Show insights.** Aggregated counts of how often ChatGPT mentions you, how many people click through to your website, and the common topics of conversations where you come up.
4. **Add extras, if you qualify.** A product feed with current prices and availability, and a "Business Agent" that answers customer questions inside ChatGPT, clearly labeled as yours.

OpenAI is also clear about what businesses don't get: "We don't share people's conversations or Business Agent chats with businesses." You see totals and topics, not transcripts.

If that sounds like Google Business Profile for ChatGPT, that's roughly right. [Nation's Restaurant News](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/chatgpt-launches-google-like-profiles-for-businesses) described it as "Google-like profiles for businesses."

## Can my South Florida business claim a ChatGPT listing yet?

Maybe. The beta is uneven, so be honest with yourself about which bucket you're in.

OpenAI's announcement says the program is "available to eligible ecommerce and online service businesses in the U.S.," and Business Agents are limited to eligible businesses. [Search Engine Journal's walkthrough](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/submit-website-chatgpt/596785/) of the help documentation adds two details that matter for local businesses: stores and restaurants "can currently claim an existing U.S. listing," and new profiles for physical locations may land on a waitlist.

In practice:

| Your business | What you can likely do today |
|---|---|
| Online store or online service (shipping, SaaS, remote consulting) | Claim or create a profile, verify, add a product feed, possibly publish a Business Agent |
| Restaurant or retail store ChatGPT already lists | Claim the existing listing and edit your details |
| Restaurant, clinic, salon, or contractor with no existing listing | Start a profile; expect a possible waitlist |
| Multi-location business | Read OpenAI's brand and location guidance before claiming each location |

If you run a café in Wilton Manors or a med spa in Boca, search for yourself in Business Hub this week. If you're listed, claim it. If you're not, start the profile anyway so you're in line.

## How do you claim your business on ChatGPT, step by step?

Based on OpenAI's setup flow as documented by [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/submit-website-chatgpt/596785/), the process looks like this:

1. **Sign in to ChatGPT and open Business Hub.** Search for your business. Claim the match, or create a new profile.
2. **Choose how customers reach you:** a physical location, a website, or both.
3. **Verify your domain.** ChatGPT gives you a TXT record to add in your DNS settings (wherever your domain is managed: GoDaddy, Cloudflare, Squarespace, and so on). Have your DNS login ready before you start; users on OpenAI's forum report the verification window is short.
4. **Pick a category** from the predefined list and write a description. Write it the way a customer would describe you, with your city and your core service in it.
5. **Add contact details** and, if you sell online, answer the delivery and return policy questions.
6. **Review your draft.**
7. **Verify your identity.** This runs through Persona and asks for your business name, DBA, business type, tax ID, website, and address. Use the exact legal name and address you use everywhere else.
8. **Check status** from the business overview. You can invite other admins on their own ChatGPT accounts, so your office manager doesn't need your password.

Claiming has no listed fee. SEJ notes OpenAI is offering an advertising credit to some businesses, but you can run an approved profile without buying ads or building an agent. I'd start there.

## Does claiming your ChatGPT profile improve your ranking?

Not directly, and OpenAI says so. Per [SEJ's reading of the help page](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/submit-website-chatgpt/596785/), claiming a business "does not guarantee higher placement," and ChatGPT keeps making its own recommendations.

That matches how these assistants already work. When someone in Fort Lauderdale asks ChatGPT for "a good family dentist near Las Olas," the answer is built from what's on the web: your website, review sites, directories, local news, and what other people say about you. A claimed profile helps ChatGPT get your facts right. It doesn't make you the answer.

What the profile does give you is something nobody had before: numbers. SEJ shared its own dashboard, showing 353,356 mentions and 2,663 website clicks over roughly four weeks. Your numbers will be much smaller. But for the first time, a local business can see whether ChatGPT talks about it at all, and what topics bring it up. That's useful for deciding what pages your website is missing.

And the audience is big enough to care about. NRN reports OpenAI says 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT every week, and that ChatGPT's search volume is now about one-fifth of Google's (outside estimates run lower). Even the conservative read says a real share of "who should I call" questions now go there first.

## ChatGPT Business Hub vs. Google Business Profile: what's different?

| | Google Business Profile | ChatGPT Business Hub |
|---|---|---|
| Status | Mature, used by nearly every local business | Beta since Nov 10, 2026 |
| Who's eligible | Businesses with in-person customer contact | Online businesses first; existing U.S. store and restaurant listings claimable |
| Verification | Video, phone, email, or postcard, depending on the business | Domain TXT record plus Persona identity check |
| Reviews | Built in | None in Business Hub; ChatGPT reads reviews from around the web |
| Insights | Searches, calls, direction requests | Mentions, website clicks, conversation topics |
| Affects ranking? | Yes, a major local factor | OpenAI says no placement guarantee |

Google Business Profile is still the bigger lever for most local businesses in Miami-Dade, Broward, and Palm Beach. Business Hub is the new one to claim before a competitor does. Do both, and make them agree.

## What should your website do now that ChatGPT has profiles?

Here's the part most "claim your profile" posts skip: the profile is a summary. Your website is the evidence. ChatGPT, like Google's AI features, leans on what it can read and confirm across sources. If your profile says "same-day AC repair in Broward" and your website never says it, you've given the model a claim with nothing behind it.

The website work is the same foundation I covered in [how local businesses get named by ChatGPT](/blog/get-cited-by-chatgpt-local-business/) and [how to get linked in Google AI Mode](/blog/google-ai-mode-website-links/). With Business Hub live, a few items move up the list:

1. **Make your name, address, phone, hours, and category identical** on your website, Google Business Profile, and ChatGPT profile.
2. **Give each core service its own page** with a plain-text first paragraph: what it is, who it's for, the price range, and the area you serve.
3. **Check your robots.txt and your host's bot settings.** If OAI-SearchBot (the crawler ChatGPT search uses) is blocked, ChatGPT is working from other people's descriptions of you. Some hosts and builders block AI crawlers by default.
4. **Put menus, price lists, and policies in real text,** not PDFs or images.
5. **Track clicks from chatgpt.com** in your analytics so you can compare them with Business Hub's numbers.
6. **Make the landing step easy.** A click from ChatGPT is someone who already got a recommendation. One-tap call, book, or quote on the first phone screen.

If your current site can't do most of that, that's the real project. A claimed profile pointing to a weak website is a nice sign on a closed door.

## What should you skip?

- **Paying someone to "rank you in ChatGPT."** OpenAI says claiming doesn't guarantee placement. Nobody can sell you a position.
- **Building a Business Agent before your basics are right.** An agent can only answer from the information you give it. Fix the facts first.
- **Writing a keyword-stuffed description.** Write it for a person. It's a summary, not a meta tag.

## FAQ

**Is it free to claim a business on ChatGPT?**
OpenAI hasn't listed a fee for claiming or managing a profile in Business Hub. Advertising and Business Agents are optional and separate from the basic profile.

**Can a local restaurant or store use ChatGPT Business Hub?**
In the November 2026 beta, U.S. stores and restaurants that ChatGPT already lists can claim their existing listing. New profiles for physical locations may go on a waitlist, while online businesses have the fullest access.

**Will claiming my ChatGPT profile make ChatGPT recommend me more?**
Not on its own. OpenAI says claiming does not guarantee higher placement, but it helps ChatGPT show accurate details and gives you data on how often you're mentioned.

**Can I see what people asked ChatGPT about my business?**
No. Business Hub shows aggregated mentions, website clicks, and common topics, and OpenAI says it doesn't share individual conversations with businesses.

**Do I still need a Google Business Profile if I claim ChatGPT?**
Yes. Google Business Profile remains a major factor in local search and Maps, and Google's AI features use it too. Keep both profiles and your website showing the same details.
