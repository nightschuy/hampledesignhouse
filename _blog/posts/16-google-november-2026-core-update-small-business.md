# Google November 2026 Core Update: What Small Businesses Should Do

**Short answer:** Google's November 2026 core update started rolling out on November 10 and may take up to two weeks, so it should wrap up right as Thanksgiving and Black Friday hit. For a small business, the right move is mostly patience: don't rewrite pages mid-rollout, compare clean weeks in Search Console, and fix the basics that help in every update. At Hample Design House in Fort Lauderdale, that's how I handle SEO for the sites I build.

Here's what Google actually said, why the timing makes your numbers hard to read this year, and a checklist for what to do (and not do) over the next few weeks.

## What is the Google November 2026 core update?

A core update is a broad change to how Google ranks pages. It isn't aimed at any one industry or trick. Google [posted the November 2026 core update](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/WLxcJyC6jMWMfryxsKMc) to its Search Status Dashboard at 10:07 a.m. Pacific on November 10, saying it "applies globally and to all languages" and that "the rollout may take up to 2 weeks to complete."

Google's announcement, [as quoted by Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-november-2026-core-update-42504.html), called it "a regular update designed to better surface relevant, satisfying content for searchers from all types of sites." That's the whole official description. No named targets, no list of affected categories.

It's the third core update of 2026, after March 27 and May 21, according to [PPC Land's rollout coverage](https://ppc.land/googles-core-update-shifts-rankings-for-up-to-2-weeks-before-black-friday/). If you've been around a few of these, the pattern is familiar: some sites go up, some go down, and most small local sites barely move.

## When will the core update finish?

Google says "up to 2 weeks," which points to roughly November 24. Don't treat that as a promise. Search Engine Roundtable keeps a table of past updates, and the [median rollout across 23 prior core updates was 14 days](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-november-2026-core-update-42504.html). Some ran long: March 2024 took 45 days. The 2026 updates were quicker, around 12 days each.

## Why does the Black Friday timing make this harder?

Because your traffic is about to change for reasons that have nothing to do with Google.

Thanksgiving is November 26, Black Friday is November 27, and Small Business Saturday is November 28. As [Search Engine Journal pointed out](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-november-2026-core-update/596614), if the rollout finishes around November 24, the first full week after it is Thanksgiving week. Compare that to a normal week and you're comparing holiday traffic to regular traffic, not "before the update" to "after."

That cuts both ways in South Florida:

- **A retail shop on Las Olas** will likely see a spike that looks like a ranking win. It's probably shoppers.
- **A B2B firm in Boca Raton** will likely see a dip as decision-makers leave for the holiday. It's probably the calendar.
- **Restaurants and hospitality** are also riding the start of season, with snowbirds arriving. More traffic in late November is normal for you every year.

None of those movements tells you what the core update did. Which brings me to the main rule.

## How do I know if the core update hurt my site?

Wait, then compare like with like. Search Engine Journal summarizes Google's advice as: [wait until the rollout is complete](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-november-2026-core-update/596614), let at least a full week pass, then compare that week with a week before the rollout started. Google's own [guidance on core updates](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-updates) is the reference document for the details.

Here's how I'd actually do it for a small business this year:

1. **Pick your "before" week now.** November 3 to 9, the last full week before the rollout began. Screenshot it in Search Console (Performance report, Search results) so you have it.
2. **Wait for Google to mark it done.** Check the status dashboard. Don't read anything into day-to-day swings while it's still rolling.
3. **Skip Thanksgiving week as your "after" week.** For most businesses, the first fair comparison will be early to mid December. Yes, that's a long wait. It's still faster than undoing a bad panic edit.
4. **Compare the same weekdays and the same pages.** Look at your top 10 pages and your top 20 queries, not just the total. A drop on one service page means something different from a drop everywhere.
5. **Check year over year if you have it.** Compare the same weeks in 2025. That's the best way to separate season from ranking.
6. **Look at leads, not just clicks.** Calls, form fills, and bookings matter more than impressions. If leads are steady and clicks fell, AI Overviews may be answering more of the question on the results page. That's a different problem from a ranking drop.

One more thing: rank-tracking tools have had a rough fall. PPC Land and Search Engine Roundtable both noted that third-party trackers have been struggling to measure ranking changes reliably. Search Console is Google's own data. Trust it over a dashboard that scrapes results.

## What should I not do during a core update?

This is where most of the damage happens, and it's self-inflicted.

- **Don't rewrite your homepage mid-rollout.** Rankings shuffle while it rolls out. Changes you make now muddy what you're measuring.
- **Don't delete pages because one dipped for three days.** Pages that drop early sometimes come back before the rollout ends.
- **Don't buy a "core update recovery" package.** A core update isn't a penalty. There's nothing to remove and no button to press.
- **Don't change your site's URLs or switch platforms in December.** Migrations always cause a temporary wobble. Doing one during an update and peak season stacks three variables on top of each other.

If you're running a holiday sale, update the content that serves customers right now: hours, offers, gift cards, holiday menus. That's normal maintenance, not a reaction to the update.

## What actually helps small business sites in a core update?

The things that help in every update. Google said this one is about surfacing "relevant, satisfying content," which for a local business mostly means proving you're real and answering the question someone searched.

1. **One clear page per service and area.** "Pool resurfacing in Pompano Beach" deserves its own page with real details, not a line in a list.
2. **Real proof.** Your own photos, your license or certifications, the neighborhoods you actually serve, the year you started. Specifics beat adjectives.
3. **Answers to the questions people ask.** Price ranges, timelines, what's included. If you won't say, an AI answer will guess. (This is the same groundwork that gets you [named by ChatGPT and other AI assistants](/blog/get-cited-by-chatgpt-local-business/).)
4. **A site that loads fast on a phone.** Local searches happen on phones, often in a hurry. A slow, cluttered page loses the visitor whatever your ranking.
5. **Consistent business details everywhere.** Name, address, phone, and hours should match on your site, Google Business Profile, and directories.
6. **A real author or owner behind the content.** A named person with a short bio is more credible than "Admin."

This matters beyond the classic blue links. Search Engine Roundtable notes that [Google integrates core ranking signals into its AI experiences](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-november-2026-core-update-42504.html), so the pages that do well in regular results are the ones AI Overviews and AI Mode tend to draw on. ## When is it a website problem, not an update problem?

If you compare clean weeks in December and your leads are down across the board, look at the site itself. Common causes I see on small business sites:

- Thin service pages that say the same thing as every competitor.
- A template site that's slow, hard to use on a phone, or full of stock photos.
- No clear way to call, book, or ask for a quote above the fold.
- Old pages that contradict current prices, hours, or services.

## FAQ

**When did the Google November 2026 core update start?**
Google started the rollout on November 10, 2026, at 10:06 a.m. Pacific, and said it may take up to two weeks. Google marks it complete on its Search Status Dashboard.

**My traffic dropped this week. Was it the core update?**
Maybe, but not necessarily. Thanksgiving, Black Friday, and the start of South Florida's season all move traffic in late November, so wait until the rollout ends and compare a clean week with November 3 to 9 or the same weeks last year.

**Is a Google core update a penalty?**
No. A core update changes how Google ranks pages overall, so there's nothing to remove or request. Sites that drop can recover over time, usually by making their pages more useful, and often see it at a later update.

**Should I change my website during the core update?**
Avoid big changes like rewrites, URL changes, or platform migrations while it's rolling out. Routine updates such as holiday hours, offers, and fixing broken links are fine.

**Does the core update affect Google AI Overviews and AI Mode?**
Google uses core ranking signals in its AI experiences, so pages that rank well in regular results are more likely to be cited in AI answers. Improving your core pages helps both.
