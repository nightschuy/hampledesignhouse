# How Small Businesses Can Use AI for Holiday Marketing in 2026

**Short answer:** Use AI for the repetitive parts of holiday marketing: drafting social posts and emails, resizing graphics, answering common questions, and updating your website's hours and offers. Keep the human parts human: your offer, your photos, your replies to real customers. At Hample Design House in Fort Lauderdale, I help South Florida businesses set this up so the website and AI tools carry the routine load during the busiest weeks of the year.

A [Verizon Business survey released November 19](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/19/3406608/0/en/verizon-survey-small-businesses-turn-to-social-media-ai-for-holidays.html) puts numbers on what I hear from owners every November. Here's what it found, and what I'd actually do with it this week.

## What are small businesses planning for the 2026 holidays?

Verizon surveyed 600 U.S. small business owners and decision-makers (companies with 1 to 500 employees) between October 30 and November 9. In Verizon's words, they're "prioritizing direct customer engagement and digital storefronts" over Black Friday and Cyber Monday.

## Why does holiday marketing burn small business owners out?

Because it all lands on the same person. The same survey found 34% of owners have pulled an all-nighter to meet holiday demands, 33% have missed or worked through a holiday, and 18% have cried over it. Half (51%) are stressed about rising costs and tariffs, and 43% about balancing work and family.

That's the real case for AI this season. Not that it makes your marketing magical. It's that a lot of holiday marketing is the same task repeated: a post for Instagram, the same post resized for Facebook, the same offer in an email, the same hours updated in four places. That's exactly the kind of work AI is good at, and exactly the kind of work that keeps an owner up at 1 a.m.

## Which holiday marketing tasks should I hand to AI?

These are the ones where AI saves real time and the downside of a mistake is small, as long as you read before you post:

1. **First drafts of social posts.** Give it your offer, dates, and tone, and ask for ten versions. Pick two, edit them in your own words.
2. **Resizing and reformatting.** One announcement into a square post, a story, an email header, and a website banner.
3. **Holiday email drafts.** A gift card reminder, a "last day to order" note, a thank-you to regulars. Short, plain, one link each.
4. **Answers to the questions you get every day.** Hours, parking, shipping cutoffs, gift card rules. Write them once (AI can help), put them on your site, and use them as saved replies.
5. **Product or menu descriptions.** Especially for seasonal items you'll only sell for six weeks.
6. **Translations for review.** In Miami and Broward, a Spanish version of your holiday post reaches more neighbors. Have a fluent person check it before it goes out.
7. **Sorting the inbox.** Tagging which messages are orders, which are questions, and which need you personally.

## What should stay human?

The parts that make people choose you over a chain:

- **Your offer.** What you're discounting, bundling, or giving away is a business decision. Don't let a tool invent a promotion.
- **Your photos.** Real photos of your shop, your team, and your product beat AI images, especially for a local business where people want to know what they'll walk into.
- **Replies to real customers.** A complaint, a special request, a thank-you from a regular. Answer those yourself.
- **Your reviews.** Never post AI-written reviews or testimonials. Ask happy customers for real ones instead.
- **Anything with a price, date, or legal promise.** Check every number in AI-written copy. Return policies and sweepstakes rules have legal requirements (this isn't legal advice; ask someone qualified).

## How does my website fit into holiday marketing?

Notice where the sales drivers point. Owners expect social posts (45%) and word of mouth (42%) to bring customers in. Both of those send people somewhere to check you out, and that somewhere is usually your Google listing and your website. A great Instagram post that lands on a site with October's hours and no gift card link wastes the work.

And 26% of owners said they'd welcome having website updates taken off their plate. I understand why. On a lot of template sites, changing your holiday hours means logging in, finding the right block, and hoping you didn't break the layout on mobile.

Here's the minimum your site should do from now through New Year's:

1. **Show holiday hours on the homepage**, plus your Google Business Profile, and make sure they match.
2. **Put the offer one tap from the top**: gift cards, holiday menu, booking, or the sale.
3. **Make contact instant**: tap to call, tap to text, or a short form that actually reaches you.
4. **Answer the top five holiday questions** on the page, so neither customers nor AI assistants have to guess. (More on that in [how to get named by ChatGPT](/blog/get-cited-by-chatgpt-local-business/).)
5. **Track what's working**: count calls, form fills, and orders, so in January you know which posts paid off.

## A 7-day AI holiday marketing plan for a small business

If you're starting this week, here's a realistic plan that fits around running the business:

| Day | Task | AI's job | Your job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Decide the offer and dates | Nothing | Decide |
| 2 | Write 10 social posts | Draft them | Edit and approve |
| 3 | Make the graphics | Resize and adapt | Supply real photos |
| 4 | Update website and Google profile | Draft the copy | Publish and check on a phone |
| 5 | Write two emails | Draft them | Edit and schedule |
| 6 | Set up saved replies | Draft answers to top questions | Approve the wording |
| 7 | Ask for reviews | Draft a short request | Send it to real customers |

That's about an hour a day. It won't replace word of mouth, which is still the second biggest sales driver in Verizon's numbers. But it buys back the evenings.

## FAQ

**How many small businesses use AI for holiday marketing?**
In Verizon Business's November 2026 survey of 600 U.S. small business owners, 45% said they plan to use AI tools to simplify holiday operations and 56% said they'll expand AI for tasks like research, marketing, and inventory.

**What is the best way to use AI for small business holiday marketing?**
Use it for first drafts and repetitive formatting: social posts, emails, resized graphics, and answers to common questions. Always edit the output and keep your offer, photos, and customer replies in your own hands.

**Can AI write my holiday social media posts?**
Yes, AI can draft them quickly, but review every post for accuracy and tone before publishing. Posts that sound like you and show real photos of your business usually perform better than generic AI copy.

**Should I update my website for the holidays?**
Yes. At minimum, show holiday hours, make the offer and contact options easy to find on a phone, and match your hours on Google Business Profile. Social posts and referrals usually send people to your site before they buy.

**Is it OK to use AI-written reviews or testimonials?**
No. Fake reviews mislead customers and break platform rules. Ask real customers for reviews instead; AI can help you write the request.
