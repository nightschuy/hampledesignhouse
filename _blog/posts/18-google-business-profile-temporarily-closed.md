# Google Business Profile Temporarily Closed: A Flood Guide for Owners

**Short answer:** If you'll be closed 6 days or fewer, set special hours on your Google Business Profile. If it's 7 days or more, mark it "Temporarily closed," and switch back to "Open with main hours" the day you reopen. Then put the same dates on your website, phone greeting, and other listings. At Hample Design House in Fort Lauderdale, I treat this as part of web design: a closure update should take ten minutes, not an afternoon.

This month was rough. Broward, Palm Beach, and Miami-Dade got hit twice, and a lot of owners are now juggling insurance calls, cleanup crews, and customers asking "are you open?" This post is the practical part: what to change, where, and in what order, so the people looking for you get a straight answer.

## What happened to South Florida businesses in November?

Two rounds of rain, two weeks apart:

- **Early November:** WLRN reported [about 13 to 15 inches between Weston and Sunrise over four days](https://www.wlrn.org/environment/2026-11-04/impassable-flooding-from-pounding-rain-is-swamping-south-florida-suburbs), with the National Weather Service calling it a "one-in-1,000-year event." Sawgrass Mills delayed opening two days in a row.
- **November 17 to 19:** Florida Realtors reported that [parts of Fort Lauderdale, Davie, and Hollywood got more than 10 inches of rain in 12 hours on November 17](https://www.floridarealtors.org/news-media/news-articles/2026/11/flood-emergency-expands-7-more-florida-counties), and the state emergency expanded on November 18 to add Broward, Miami-Dade, and Monroe, for 21 counties in total.
- **The business side:** CBS12 reported [nearly 3,000 customers without power and all Broward County government offices closed](https://cbs12.com/news/local/florida-weather-news-broward-county-declares-state-of-emergency-after-overnight-flooding-nearly-3000-power-outages-flooding-roads-traffic-signals-) on November 18. WLRN's reporting, [republished by WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/weather/2026-11-19/i-have-never-seen-rain-like-this-days-of-downpour-mess-with-businesses-schools), described a Fort Lauderdale law office closed two days and a Weston salon whose stylists couldn't get to work. NBC 6 reported a Davie gym facing [equipment damage that "could approach $200,000"](https://www.nbcmiami.com/weather/stories-weather/flooding-fort-lauderdale-davie-damage/3890702/).

Some of those closures lasted a day. Some will last weeks. Google treats those two situations differently, and picking the wrong one confuses customers.

## Should I mark my Google Business Profile temporarily closed or set special hours?

Google's own help pages draw the line at about a week. The [special hours page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076?hl=en) says to use special hours when you "remain closed for up to 6 days in a row." The [closure page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300196?hl=en) says to use Temporarily closed when you're closed "for more than 7 days," for the off-season, or indefinitely.

Here's how I'd map it:

| Your situation | On Google | On your website |
|---|---|---|
| Closed a day or two, reopening on a known date | Special hours, marked Closed for those dates | A one-line banner with the reopen date |
| Open with reduced hours during cleanup | Special hours with the actual hours for each day | Banner plus updated hours on your contact page |
| Closed 7+ days, or reopen date unknown | Temporarily closed | Banner, a short update page, and a way to reach you |
| Open, but a service is paused (delivery, walk-ins) | Keep main hours; update "More hours" or post an update | Note it on the affected service page |
| Moving to a new location | Don't reopen the old profile; follow Google's move steps | New address everywhere, old address redirected |
| Not reopening | Permanently closed | Tell people where to go instead |

Two details that trip people up:

1. **Special hours only show if your profile is set to "Open with main hours."** Google says so directly on the special hours page. If you'd hidden your hours, they won't appear.
2. **Never pick Permanently closed for a temporary problem.** Google's [reopen page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/15300571?hl=en) warns that if a profile was marked permanently closed as part of a move or name change, you shouldn't reopen it; you create and verify a new profile instead, and moving reviews over takes a support request.

## Does marking temporarily closed hurt my Google ranking?

A little, temporarily, and only in some searches. Google's wording: "For broad queries, temporarily-closed businesses can rank after open businesses." That depends on your category, the search, and how many competitors are also closed. When someone searches your name, the label is the point: they see you exist and the closure is temporary.

Joy Hawkins at Sterling Sky, who has watched Google's local results for years, adds some practical notes in her [guide to the Temporarily closed label](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/temporarily-closed-google-business-profile/): temporarily closed listings can still receive reviews, the label doesn't carry over to Google Ads location extensions, and you shouldn't set a future "opening date" while closed because it can hide the listing.

My take: if you're truly closed for weeks, take the small ranking dip. Sending people to a locked door does more damage than ranking third for a week. If you'll be back in a few days, special hours keep you in full standing.

## How do I update Google Business Profile hours, step by step?

From Google's help pages, as of this week:

1. Search your business name on Google while signed in, or go to business.google.com, and choose **Edit profile**, then **Hours**.
2. **Short closure:** next to **Special hours**, choose **Edit**, add each date, and leave **Closed** checked (or uncheck it and enter reduced hours). Save.
3. **Longer closure:** next to **Hours**, choose **Edit**, select **Temporarily closed**, and save.
4. **Several locations:** in Business Profile Manager, select the locations, then **Actions**, then **Mark as temporarily closed**.
5. **Reopening:** back under **Hours**, choose **Open with main hours**, check your days, confirm the times, and save.

Then post a short update on the profile with what's happening and the date you expect to reopen. People trust a dated note.

## What should my website say while I'm closed?

Your Google profile and your website need to agree. DreamHost checked [230 local businesses and found 53% had at least one missing or mismatched detail](https://www.dreamhost.com/blog/google-business-profile/) between their Google profile and their site, with hours the most common mismatch (27%). That's in normal times. After a flood, it gets worse, because people update one place and forget the rest.

A closure update on your site should include:

- **A banner on every page** with the status and the date: "Closed through December 5 after flood damage. Call or text for urgent requests."
- **One way to reach you that works.** If the office line is down, give a cell number or a form that goes to your phone.
- **What still works.** Online orders, phone consults, a partner location, gift cards.
- **Updated hours in your structured data.** If your site has LocalBusiness schema with opening hours, update it too, or search engines and AI assistants will read the old hours.

If changing a banner on your site means emailing a developer and waiting, that's the real problem to fix. A status banner should be something you can switch on yourself, from your phone, in a minute.

## Where else do customers check if I'm open?

More places than they used to. Update these in the same sitting:

1. **Google Business Profile** (above).
2. **Your website** (above).
3. **Phone greeting and voicemail.** Say the reopen date. If calls go unanswered, an automatic text-back is cheap insurance; I covered the setup in [where AI actually pays first for Florida small businesses](/blog/ai-lead-response-small-business-florida/).
4. **Apple Maps (Business Connect), Bing Places, and Yelp.** Each has its own hours and closure setting.
5. **ChatGPT.** If you've claimed your business there, update it. If you haven't, [here's how to claim your business on ChatGPT](/blog/claim-business-on-chatgpt/). AI assistants describe you from whatever they can find, and stale hours are a common way they get it wrong (more in [how local businesses get named by ChatGPT](/blog/get-cited-by-chatgpt-local-business/)).
6. **Social profiles and pinned posts.** One pinned post beats ten stories that disappear.
7. **Delivery and booking apps.** Pause them, or customers will order from a closed kitchen.

If your business took physical damage, the state runs a [Business Damage Assessment Survey](https://floridadisaster.biz/BusinessDamageAssessments) after major disasters to measure impact and shape relief programs. Check whether it's active for this event; filling it out takes a few minutes. (Not legal or insurance advice, just a pointer.)

## How do I reopen without losing ground?

The day you reopen:

1. Switch Google back to **Open with main hours** and delete any leftover special hours.
2. Take the banner down, or change it to "We're open" for a week.
3. Post a reopening update on Google with a photo of the space.
4. Ask a few happy regulars for a review. Fresh reviews tell Google, and people, that you're operating.
5. Reset your phone greeting and turn delivery and booking apps back on.

Then write down what you changed and where, as a short list. Hurricane season comes back in June, and next time you'll do this in ten minutes.

## FAQ

**How long can I use special hours on Google Business Profile?**
Google says special hours are for adjusted hours or closures of up to 6 days in a row. For 7 days or more, it recommends marking the business Temporarily closed.

**Will customers see that I'm temporarily closed on Google Maps?**
Yes. Google shows a "Temporarily closed" label on your profile on both Search and Maps, so people searching your name know you still exist and plan to reopen.

**Can I still get Google reviews while my business is temporarily closed?**
According to Sterling Sky's guide, temporarily closed listings can still receive reviews. Only a permanently closed listing stops them.

**What should I put on my website if flooding closed my business?**
A banner on every page with your status and expected reopen date, one contact method that works right now, and anything customers can still do, like online orders or phone consults. Update the hours in your site's structured data too.

**How do I reopen my Google Business Profile after a temporary closure?**
Go to Edit profile, then Hours, choose "Open with main hours," confirm your days and times, and save. Remove any leftover special hours so they don't override your regular schedule.
