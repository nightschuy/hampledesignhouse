# GoDaddy Airo AI Builder vs. Cloudways vs. Hostinger (Nov 2026)

**Short answer:** GoDaddy Airo AI Builder, the Cloudways AI Website Builder, and Hostinger's AI tools all launched or expanded in November 2026, and all three build a site from a chat. The difference that matters is ownership: Cloudways outputs standard WordPress you can move, GoDaddy lets paid plans download the code, and Hostinger's new Headless hosting keeps code in your hands. At Hample Design House in Miami and Fort Lauderdale, that exit door is the first thing I check on any website builder.

Two weeks ago I looked at [Bluehost's AI Builder](/blog/bluehost-ai-builder-small-business/). Since then, three more hosting companies have shipped their own. That's not a coincidence. Hosting companies want you building inside their walls, because a site that can't leave pays rent forever.

So instead of reviewing each one in isolation, here's the comparison a small business owner actually needs: what it builds, what it costs, and whether you can take it with you.

## What launched in November?

- **Cloudways AI Website Builder (November 16).** DigitalOcean's Cloudways [announced a builder](https://investors.digitalocean.com/news/news-details/2026/Cloudways-Launches-AI-Website-Builder-Enabling-Businesses-To-Create-And-Launch-WordPress-Websites-In-Minutes/default.aspx) that turns a text prompt into a WordPress or WooCommerce site built with native Gutenberg blocks. It writes copy, picks images, sets up basic SEO, and includes both a visual and a code editor. Cloudways says builds took "approximately 4 minutes on average, with a 98% success rate" in its public preview.
- **GoDaddy Airo AI Builder (November 17 coverage).** TechRadar [reported on GoDaddy's conversational builder](https://www.techradar.com/pro/godaddys-new-ai-builder-app-lets-smbs-turn-an-idea-into-a-website-faster-than-ever) for websites, web apps, stores, and booking systems, with more than 90 third-party integrations at launch (Slack, Notion, HubSpot) and Stripe for payments. It also reported that service businesses made up over a third of the sites created.
- **Hostinger Headless (November 27).** Hostinger [launched Headless](https://www.techradar.com/pro/website-building/infrastructure-now-has-to-make-sense-to-agents-not-just-to-people-hostinger-launches-headless-to-tie-websites-to-the-backend-in-this-new-ai-age), which sets up the backend, hosting, and deployment for projects built with AI coding tools, and said "more than 1,000 sites were set up in over a week." It sits next to Hostinger's [AI Builder](https://www.hostinger.com/blog/ai-builder-launch/), which went global in August and builds from a prompt, a file, a screenshot, or a URL.

## What does an AI website builder really cost over two years?

The sticker price is the wrong number. Two things move the real cost:

1. **Renewals.** Hostinger's Unlimited plan is $2.79 a month on a 48-month term, paid upfront ($133.92 today), and renews at $16.99 a month. That's still cheap, but it's six times the headline.
2. **Stacking.** The Cloudways builder is $7.99 per website a month, and it requires a Cloudways hosting plan billed separately. The promo price of $1.60 for the first two months runs November 3 to December 4.

Then there's the cost that never shows on a pricing page: your hours. An AI builder gets you a draft in minutes. Getting that draft to say the right things, look like your brand, and turn visitors into calls is still work. If you're comparing against a studio, [here's what a website costs in Fort Lauderdale and Miami](/blog/website-cost-fort-lauderdale-miami/) so you can line up the numbers honestly.

## Can you export a site made with an AI website builder?

This is the question I'd ask first, and the answers are better than I expected:

- **Cloudways:** the strongest answer of the three. The site is standard WordPress, and Cloudways says it can be exported. Any WordPress developer can pick it up later.
- **GoDaddy Airo:** GoDaddy's page says "Download your code with any paid plan." Free-plan users can't download. Git integration is listed as coming soon.
- **Hostinger:** Headless is built for code you made with tools like Claude Code or Cursor, so you already hold the code. For Hostinger's AI Builder itself, I didn't find a clear export statement on the pages I read.

Compare that with the Bluehost review two weeks ago, where an early tester [found no export option at all](/blog/bluehost-ai-builder-small-business/). Exportable code is the difference between "I can switch hosts in an afternoon" and "I have to rebuild."

One caveat: code you can download isn't the same as a site you can easily run elsewhere. A web app with logins, a database, and payments still needs somewhere to live and someone who can set it up. Ask what comes with the export: just the pages, or the data too.

## Which AI builder fits which business?

I'll be fair to all three. Each has a real use:

- **GoDaddy Airo AI Builder** fits a service business that wants a booking page or quote form wired into tools it already uses, like HubSpot or Stripe, and doesn't want to think about hosting.
- **Cloudways** fits a business that wants WordPress (huge plugin ecosystem, lots of people who can maintain it) and wants AI to do the first draft. It's the most portable choice here.
- **Hostinger Headless** fits someone already building with an AI coding tool who needs a cheap place to put the result. It's not a builder for non-technical owners on its own.

And there's a fourth option. For a business where the website's job is to win new customers through search and AI answers, I still think a designed site you own outright beats all of them. I wrote about the trade-offs in [AI website builder vs. hiring a designer](/blog/ai-website-builder-vs-designer/). The short version: builders give you speed, and a designer gives you a site that doesn't look like the other five med spas in Brickell.

## What should you check before you pick any AI builder?

Run this list on any builder, including ones that launch next month:

1. **Exit:** can you download the code and your form or customer data on the plan you'll actually be on?
2. **Domain:** is the domain registered in your name, and does it stay yours if you cancel? A "free domain for life" tied to a plan is free only while you keep paying.
3. **Renewal price:** what's the monthly price after the intro term ends?
4. **Search settings:** after publishing, check for a noindex tag and open yoursite.com/robots.txt to see whether AI crawlers like GPTBot and ClaudeBot are blocked.
5. **Brand:** does it look like you, or like the template everyone else picked?
6. **Tracking:** can you count calls, forms, and bookings, so you know whether the site works?

If a builder passes all six, use it. If it fails the first two, treat whatever you build there as temporary.

## FAQ

**Is GoDaddy Airo AI Builder free?**
There's a free plan with 50 AI credits a month, and credits don't roll over. Downloading your code requires a paid plan, and GoDaddy's free domain offer depends on keeping an annual plan.

**How much does the Cloudways AI Website Builder cost?**
Cloudways lists it at $7.99 per website per month, on top of a Cloudways hosting plan billed separately. A promo price of $1.60 for the first two months runs through December 4, 2026.

**Can I move a site built with an AI website builder to another host?**
It depends on the builder. Cloudways produces standard WordPress that can be exported, GoDaddy Airo lets paid plans download the code, and some other builders offer no export at all, so check before you build.

**What is Hostinger Headless?**
Hostinger Headless, launched in November 2026, provides hosting, a backend, and deployment for websites and apps built with AI coding tools. It's included with Hostinger's Unlimited and Cloud plans.

**Should a small business in Miami use an AI builder or a web designer?**
Use an AI builder to test an idea, for internal tools, or when budget rules everything else. Hire a designer when the site needs to win customers through search, stand out from competitors, and belong to you.
