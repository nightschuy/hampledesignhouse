Here's what changed, what it actually means for a local business, and the short list of fixes worth doing this month.

What did Google change in AI Mode on October 15, 2026?

Google announced five changes to AI Mode and AI Overviews in a post on its Keyword blog:

  1. More inline links. Google says it has made "continued and substantial increases" to links inside AI responses, and will place them higher in the answer more often.
  2. An official-sites section (in testing). For some tasks, Google will show the one or two official sites at the top of the response, with a note on why each is useful.
  3. More news carousels. Carousels of timely articles are expanding to more places, languages, and queries, and they favor sources people have picked as Preferred Sources. Google says Preferred Sources use has grown more than 7x since the start of the year.
  4. Better "related articles" links. Links at the end of some answers now get image thumbnails and a more clickable design.
  5. Clickable creator names (in testing). On video results in AI Mode, creator and publisher names now link to their Google Search profiles.

Items 3 to 5 mostly matter to publishers. Items 1 and 2 matter to anyone with a website and a phone number.

Will Google AI Mode actually send clicks to my website?

Some, and probably fewer than you'd hope. Be clear-eyed about this.

When Pew Research tracked the browsing of 900 U.S. adults in March 2025, people clicked a regular search result on 8% of visits to a results page with an AI summary, versus 15% without one. They clicked a link inside the AI summary itself on just 1% of visits.

Google's update is partly an answer to that. But nobody outside Google can measure the effect yet. As Search Engine Land's Barry Schwartz noted, Search Console still doesn't report AI Overview clicks separately.

So the practical math for a local business is this: the answer gets read far more often than any link gets clicked. Two things follow.

  • What the answer says about you matters as much as the link. Your name, your service, your city, your hours.
  • When someone does click, they're close to buying. A person who reads an AI answer and still taps through usually wants to book, order, call, or check a detail. Your site has to make that step easy in one tap.

What is Google's "official sites" section, and who gets in?

Google describes it as a test: when there are only one or two official sources for a task or a specific fact, those sites get their own section at the top of the AI response. Google frames it as an extension of something it already does, showing a site prominently when you're clearly looking for that site.

Google hasn't said how it picks the sites, and it didn't mention local businesses, Maps, or Business Profiles. So here's my read, clearly labeled as a read: for a local business, the "tasks" that fit this pattern are things like booking a table, ordering, scheduling an appointment, checking a menu or a price list, or getting your hours. There is exactly one official source for "book at your restaurant." The question is whether Google thinks it's you.

A hypothetical that comes up constantly: a restaurant on Las Olas whose website has no menu in text (just a PDF), whose "Reserve" button jumps to a third-party platform, and whose hours on the site disagree with its Google Business Profile. Ask an AI assistant how to book there and the most useful, consistent source it can find may be a directory or a delivery app, not the restaurant. The restaurant gets mentioned. Someone else gets the click.

How do you get linked in Google AI Mode?

Google's own guidance is refreshingly short. Its documentation on AI features says there are "no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." A page just has to be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet. Google also notes that AI Mode may run several related searches across subtopics (it calls this "query fan-out") to build one answer, which is why a site with clear pages for each service gets more chances to be pulled in.

The best practices Google lists are the unglamorous ones: don't block crawling in robots.txt or at your hosting or CDN layer, make content easy to find through internal links, put important content in text, keep structured data matching what's visible on the page, and keep your Business Profile and Merchant Center details current.

Here's how that maps onto the update:

None of this is new SEO. It's the foundation from how local businesses get named by ChatGPT. What's new is that Google now visibly rewards the site that is the definitive source for a task.

Your 15-minute official-site audit

  1. Search Google for "[your business name] hours" and "[your business name] booking" in AI Mode. Note which sites get linked, and whether yours is one of them.
  2. Open your homepage on your phone. Can you call, book, or get directions in one tap from the first screen?
  3. Compare your hours, phone, and address on your site against your Google Business Profile. Fix any mismatch today.
  4. Find anything important that only exists as an image or a PDF (menus, price sheets, service areas). Put it on the page as text.
  5. Check that your booking or ordering flow starts on your own domain, even if a third-party tool runs underneath.
  6. Make sure every core service has its own page whose first paragraph answers "what is it, who is it for, what does it cost, where."
  7. Open your robots.txt and your host or CDN bot settings. Confirm Googlebot isn't blocked.

If step 2 or 3 failed, start there. Those are free fixes with the most direct payoff.

What to skip

  • "AI Mode optimization" packages. Google says no special optimization exists for AI features. Anyone selling a secret method is selling ordinary SEO at a premium, or something worse.
  • Special schema just for AI. Google says there's no special schema.org markup required. Keep the structured data you have accurate and matched to the visible page.
  • Panic rebuilds. If your site is fast, crawlable, and accurate, this update helps you. Fix the gaps from the audit before you spend real money.

Where your website fits in

FAQ

Does my business need special SEO to show up in Google AI Mode?

No. Google says there are no extra requirements or special optimizations for AI Overviews or AI Mode. Your pages need to be indexed, eligible to show with a snippet, and genuinely useful, which is standard SEO.

What are the official sites Google shows at the top of AI Mode answers?

It's a test Google announced on October 15, 2026: for tasks or facts with only one or two official sources, Google highlights those sites at the top of the response with a short note on why each helps. Google hasn't published how sites are selected.

Do people click links in Google's AI answers?

Less than on regular results. Pew Research found people clicked a link inside an AI summary on about 1% of visits, though Google has since added more and higher-placed links. The people who do click tend to be ready to act.

Does my Google Business Profile affect AI Mode?

Google's guidance tells site owners to keep Business Profile information current for its AI features. Matching your hours, phone, and address between your profile and your website removes a common reason to trust another source over you.

How long does it take to start showing up in AI answers after fixing my site?

Fixes like matching your hours or adding text content can be picked up as soon as Google recrawls the page, often within days to a few weeks. Showing up for competitive category questions takes longer and depends on your rankings and reputation.
