Shopify Canvas: Should You Build Your Store by Chatting With AI?

Shopify made the code part easy. Its own design director says the hard part was the taste.

Short answer: Shopify Canvas, launched October 1, 2026, lets merchants design their store by chatting with Shopify's AI agent, Sidekick, on a live preview of real store code. It's a strong tool for owners who already know what their brand should look like. It's early access, desktop-only, and can't yet handle third-party themes, app blocks, or translations. At Hample Design House in Miami and Fort Lauderdale, I'd use it for changes, not for deciding what your store should be.

Here's what Canvas actually does, what it can't do yet, and how to decide whether it replaces a designer for your store.

What is Shopify Canvas?

Canvas is a new design surface inside Shopify. Instead of the classic theme editor, where you edit one page at a time through a sidebar of sections and blocks, Canvas lays out every page of your store together on one zoomable workspace. You can click an element and edit it, or describe what you want to Sidekick in chat and watch the change land in real time.

The interesting part is under the hood. Shopify says the preview is your real, working store code, with interactions, animations, different products, and different screen sizes, not a static mockup. Sidekick edits the theme files directly, validates its own code, and takes screenshots of its work to check it before handing it back. Shopify also simplified its theme architecture so the AI can understand and change it more easily, according to TechCrunch.

Shopify is not new to this. In its launch post, the company says Sidekick made more than 25 million edits to themes in the first half of 2026 alone. Ben Sehl, Shopify's Director of Product, put the pitch plainly: "Today, an entrepreneur on Shopify can build a fully custom store in twenty minutes."

Who can use Shopify Canvas right now?

Not everyone yet. Per Shopify's Canvas requirements page, it's in early access and available only to certain stores, though the launch changelog says it's rolling out to existing stores. To use it:

  1. You need a desktop. Canvas doesn't run on phones or tablets.
  2. Your plan must include theme customization. If you later drop to a plan that doesn't, you can view Canvas-edited themes but not edit or publish them.
  3. Your theme must be made by Shopify or custom-built. Third-party themes bought from the Shopify Theme Store aren't supported.
  4. Anyone using it needs the staff permission to edit theme code.

If you're starting fresh, the platform itself is the familiar math: Shopify's pricing page lists the Basic plan at $39 a month billed monthly ($29 a month billed yearly), with online card rates starting at 2.9% plus 30 cents.

What can't Shopify Canvas do yet?

This is the section most launch coverage skimmed. Shopify is upfront about it, and the limits matter more than the demo.

Launch limit (per Shopify) Why it matters for a small store
Themes edited in Canvas don't receive theme updates Fixes and improvements Shopify ships to your base theme won't reach your Canvas copy unless someone applies them by hand
You can't download theme files for a Canvas-edited theme No local backup and no clean handoff to a developer who works in code
No app blocks or app embeds Reviews widgets, subscriptions, loyalty, and booking apps that live in theme blocks have to be configured elsewhere
No direct translation Translated content goes through Shopify's Translate & Adapt app, not Canvas
No per-market customization You can't tailor the theme for different Shopify Markets inside Canvas
Third-party themes not supported If you bought a premium theme from the Theme Store, you can't open it in Canvas
Desktop only Quick fixes from your phone stay in the classic tools

Shopify says it plans to add third-party themes, Markets, translations, and app blocks over time, and Sehl himself said "we're early here and Canvas is not replacing the existing editor yet." Your original theme stays in the classic editor, and Canvas works on a new or duplicated theme, so trying it is low risk.

The Miami detail

For a lot of stores in Miami-Dade and Broward, a Spanish version of the storefront isn't a nice extra. It's half the market. Canvas can't translate theme content directly or customize per market at launch, so a bilingual store still needs Translate & Adapt (or a person) to get the Spanish side right. AI can draft that translation in seconds. Someone who knows how your customers actually talk should still read it before it goes live.

Is Shopify Canvas good enough to replace a web designer?

For some stores, yes. I said the same about AI website builders in AI website builder vs. hiring a designer, and Canvas is a better tool than most of them because it edits real theme code instead of stacking a page builder on top.

But one quote from Shopify's own launch post is the honest answer to this question. Austin Knight, a design director at Shopify, said: "Getting Sidekick to write code was easy," while "guiding it to make good design decisions took a great deal of time and attention."

That's Shopify's design team describing the bottleneck, and it's the same one you'll hit. Canvas will build exactly what you ask for, quickly. It won't tell you that your hero photo makes a $90 product look like a $30 one, that your navigation buries the collection that makes money, or that your store looks like the three others in your niche that typed a similar prompt. As I wrote in web design in 2027, when AI makes everything fast, originality becomes the moat.

When should you use Canvas, and when should you hire a designer?

Your situation Use Canvas yourself Bring in a designer
You already have a clear brand (logo, type, colors, photos) Yes. Canvas is great at applying a system you already have Only for a specific problem, like conversion or speed
New store, no brand yet You'll get a store in an afternoon. It will look like a template Yes. Brand first, then build. Canvas can handle upkeep after
Seasonal updates, new collections, promo banners Yes. This is the sweet spot No
Premium pricing that needs to look premium Possible, if you have a strong eye Usually. The look is part of the price
Bilingual storefront or multiple markets Not yet inside Canvas Yes, or plan the translation workflow separately
Heavy app setup (subscriptions, bookings, reviews) Partly. Apps still configure outside Canvas Helpful for the first setup

The split I'd recommend to most small brands: pay once for the decisions (brand identity, layout, what the store leads with), then run day-to-day changes yourself in Canvas. That's the AI-accelerated version of how a good site has always worked, and it's how I approach my own process in how I ship a website in days, not months.

Before you open Canvas: a 6-step checklist

  1. Duplicate, don't edit. Open a duplicate of your live theme in Canvas so the original stays in the classic editor as your fallback.
  2. List your theme apps. Write down every app that adds a block or embed to your storefront. Those won't be configurable in Canvas.
  3. Note your theme version. A Canvas copy stops receiving theme updates, so know what version you started from.
  4. Write the brief before the prompt. Three sentences on who buys from you, what they should feel, and the one action you want. Paste it into Sidekick first.
  5. Check every change on a real phone. Canvas is desktop-only, and most of your customers aren't.
  6. Decide who owns translation. If you sell in English and Spanish, plan that workflow outside Canvas for now.

FAQ

What is Shopify Canvas?

Canvas is a design workspace in Shopify, launched October 1, 2026, that shows every page of your store at once and lets you edit it by clicking or by chatting with Sidekick, Shopify's AI agent. Changes render on your real store code in real time.

Is Shopify Canvas free?

Shopify hasn't announced a separate fee. Canvas is available on Shopify plans that include theme customization, so the cost is your regular Shopify subscription.

Can I use Shopify Canvas with a theme I bought from the Theme Store?

Not at launch. Canvas works only with themes developed by Shopify and with custom themes. Shopify says third-party theme support is planned.

Will Canvas break my current store?

It shouldn't. Canvas works on a new or duplicated theme, and your original theme stays in the existing editor. Just know that a Canvas-edited theme stops receiving theme updates and can't be downloaded.

Can Shopify Canvas make my store bilingual?

Not directly. At launch, Canvas can't translate theme content or customize per market, so Shopify points merchants to its Translate & Adapt app for translations.
